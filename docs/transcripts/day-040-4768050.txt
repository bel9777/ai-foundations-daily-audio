The Mental Model

Think of retrieval as a library visit with three jobs. First comes chunking. A long document is divided into smaller pieces called chunks. A chunk should keep one coherent idea together, such as a return window or a specific exception. Large chunks carry unrelated noise. Tiny chunks can lose the context needed to understand a rule. Good chunking is the middle ground: small enough to find, yet large enough to make sense. Second comes search. A person asks a question, and the system gathers a shortlist of chunks that might help. Keyword search looks for matching words. Semantic search looks for similar meaning, so a question about ending a membership can find text about canceling a subscription. Search is fast, but it is not perfect. Third comes reranking. The shortlist may hold ten or fifty candidates. Reranking compares the question and each candidate more carefully, then places the strongest evidence on top. Search is a librarian pulling books from shelves. Reranking is opening the books and placing the most useful page first. Chunking prepares material, search finds candidates, and reranking improves the selection before the model writes. Poor chunks make search noisy. Weak search misses useful evidence. No reranking can leave the model with relevant material, but not the most directly relevant material. A Real-World Example

Imagine a customer-support assistant for an online store. Its help center contains pages about shipping, returns, warranties, payment, and special cases. A customer asks how long they have to send back a damaged item. The system should not paste every page into the prompt. It chunks the pages into sections, perhaps separating a general return rule from a damaged-item exception. Search finds pieces about returns, damage, and deadlines. Reranking promotes the specific damaged-item deadline above a broad ordinary-returns page. The AI can then answer from the most relevant source instead of mixing policies. A huge chunk might bury the exception in noise. A tiny chunk might separate the deadline from the condition that makes it apply. Recap and One Action

Three takeaways. First, chunking balances focus and context. Second, search finds a fast shortlist using words or meaning. Third, reranking makes the final evidence set more precise. Together, they improve the material available before an AI generates an answer. One action: take one document you use often and mark where you would split it into meaningful sections.
