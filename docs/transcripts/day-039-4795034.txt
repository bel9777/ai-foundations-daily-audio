The Mental Model

Retrieval-Augmented Generation is a pattern with three important ideas. Retrieval means searching documents, notes, web pages, or records for useful information. Context is the text the model can see while answering. Generation is the model writing a response. An answer is grounded when it is based on provided source material rather than only the model’s memory. In RAG, the model receives a packet of relevant evidence before it writes. Large language models are good at language, but they are not perfect databases. They can be outdated, incomplete, or confidently wrong. RAG helps by supplying current or domain-specific information at answer time. It is useful for changing policies, product details, schedules, private documents, or explanations of a particular source. But RAG is not a truth machine. A Real-World Example

Imagine an internal policy assistant. Employees ask about vacation rules, expense limits, or onboarding instructions. The policy manual is the source material. A RAG system searches it, retrieves the passages most related to the question, and asks the model to answer using only those passages. This is better than hoping the model memorized a policy correctly, and it is faster than asking an employee to read the full manual every time. For a question about a meal expense, the system should retrieve the exact policy section rather than a broad description of benefits. The retrieved passages become the evidence; the generated answer explains that evidence in plain English.
