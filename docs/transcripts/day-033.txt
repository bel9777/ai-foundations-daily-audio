AI Foundations — Day 33: Sampling, Temperature, and Output Diversity

Day 33: Sampling, Temperature, and Output Diversity Objective: By the end of today’s lesson, you’ll understand why the same AI model can give different answers, how sampling works, what temperature changes, and how to choose the right level of output diversity for a task. Prerequisites: You should already be comfortable with tokens and context windows (Day 27), and you should know that large language models predict the next token one step at a time (Day 29). It also helps to remember that models can be trained further with instruction following and human feedback (Days 30–31). 1) Why models do not always say the same thing When a language model answers a prompt, it does not usually have only one possible next word. Instead, it assigns a score to many possible next tokens and then chooses among them. This choice process is called sampling when the model selects from several likely options rather than always picking the single top choice. Think of it this way: the model has a bucket of possible next words, and some are more likely than others. Sampling decides how to draw from that bucket. If the model always picked the single most likely token, it would be more predictable but also more repetitive. If it sampled more freely, it could become more varied, creative, or surprising. 2) Temperature: the knob that changes variety Temperature is a setting that controls how “focused” or “spread out” the model’s choices are during sampling. Lower temperature makes the model stick closer to the most likely tokens. Higher temperature makes it more willing to choose less likely tokens. Low temperature = safer, more consistent, less varied High temperature = more diverse, more surprising, less predictable A useful mental model is a thermostat for creativity. A colder setting narrows the options; a warmer setting widens them. The model does not become “smarter” or “dumber” from temperature alone. It simply changes how much randomness is allowed in the output. Important term: randomness means the model may choose between several plausible options instead of always repeating the same one. This is not chaos; it is controlled variation. 3) Output diversity: what you gain, and what you risk Output diversity means how different the model’s answers are from one run to the next. A highly diverse model may give many distinct phrasings, ideas, or story endings. A low-diversity model tends to repeat similar wording and structure. Diversity is useful when you want brainstorming, creative writing, slogan ideas, or multiple solution paths. Lower diversity is useful when you want consistency, summaries, factual extraction, or customer-support style replies that should sound steady and reliable. But there is a tradeoff: More diversity can help generate fresh ideas. Too much diversity can make answers drift away from the prompt. So temperature is not a “better or worse” setting. It is a task choice. 4) A memorable analogy: the restaurant menu Imagine a restaurant with a huge menu. The chef has many possible dishes that fit your order. Sampling is the chef’s choice process. Temperature is how adventurous the chef feels. At low temperature, the chef picks the safest, most standard dish that best matches your request. At higher temperature, the chef is more willing to try a less obvious but still reasonable option. If the chef gets too adventurous, you may get something interesting but not quite what you wanted. This is why the same prompt can produce different answers. The model is not “changing its mind”; it is drawing from possibilities in a controlled way. 5) Concrete real-world example Suppose you ask a model for a product slogan for a water bottle. At low temperature , you might get: Drink better. Carry lighter. At higher temperature , you might get: Hydration that keeps up with your day. At an even higher temperature, you might get something more unusual, which could be creative or could feel off-topic. If you are writing a legal summary, you probably want low diversity. If you are generating ad copy or fiction prompts, you may want more diversity. 6) How this connects to earlier lessons Sampling happens one token at a time, which ties directly back to next-token prediction from Day 29. The model still builds each answer sequentially from token probabilities. Sampling also depends on the context window from Day 27, because the model can only use the text currently in view when deciding what comes next. If earlier parts of a long conversation fall out of the context window, the model may sample differently because it is working with less information. Finally, instruction following and human feedback from Days 30–31 influence the shape of those probabilities. In other words, training makes the model more likely to answer helpfully, and sampling chooses among those helpful possibilities. 7) A simple rule of thumb Use low temperature for consistency, precision, and factual tasks. Use medium temperature for balanced conversation and general writing. Use higher temperature for brainstorming and creative exploration. If a response feels too repetitive, raise the temperature a little. If it feels too loose or unreliable, lower it. Hands-on exercise: compare outputs for yourself You do not need paid tools for this. Use any free chat model you can access, or even a local notebook if you have one. The key is to run the same prompt multiple times and compare the results. Try this prompt: Write three different one-sentence explanations of what a neural network is for a complete beginner. Now do one of the following: If your tool lets you change temperature, try low and then higher settings. If you cannot change temperature, run the same prompt several times and compare how much the outputs vary. As you compare responses, note: Which version sounds most consistent? Which version sounds most creative? Which version is easiest to trust for a factual explanation? Optional extension: Try a creative prompt like Give me five names for a tiny robot that waters plants. Notice how higher diversity may be helpful there. Knowledge check 1. What is sampling in a language model? 2. What does lowering temperature usually do to the model’s output? 3. When would you want higher output diversity? ------------------------------ Answers 1. Sampling is the process of choosing the next token from several possible options instead of always picking the single most likely one. 2.
