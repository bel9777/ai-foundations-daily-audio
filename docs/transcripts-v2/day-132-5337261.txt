Jordan: What is the difference between a loss function and an evaluation metric, and why train on log loss if you evaluate on recall at a set threshold?
Alex: The loss function has to be smooth and differentiable so optimization math like gradient descent can minimize it during training. The evaluation metric is the harsh business reality, which is often step-like or non-differentiable. Log loss forces the model to output well-calibrated probabilities, and then you pick your operational threshold later based on error costs.
Jordan: Correct. You train with what the math requires, but you evaluate based on what the real-world operation demands.

Jordan: Perfect. When running out of cash hurts twice as much as holding surplus, your optimal operational forecast deliberately aims high rather than guessing down the middle.
