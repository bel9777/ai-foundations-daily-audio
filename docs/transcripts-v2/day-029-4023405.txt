It's a form of supervised learning, but we don't need humans to label the data because the correct answers are already right there in the original text.

Jordan: Because human text isn't random noise. Language written by people has deep underlying structure. To predict the next token accurately across billions of sentences, a model can't just memorize word pairs. It has to pick up on grammar, factual knowledge about the world, tone, and logical reasoning patterns.
Alex: Give me an everyday analogy for that, because it still feels like a massive leap.
